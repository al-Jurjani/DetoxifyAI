# Professional Communication Style Guide

## Core Principles

### 1. Preserve Intent, Change Tone
- Maintain the original message's purpose
- Remove emotional charge and hostility
- Keep factual content intact

### 2. Use "I" Statements
**Avoid:** "You always mess up"
**Use:** "I've noticed this pattern"

### 3. Focus on Behavior, Not Person
**Avoid:** "You're incompetent"
**Use:** "This work doesn't meet our standards"

### 4. Offer Solutions, Not Just Criticism
**Avoid:** "This is terrible"
**Use:** "This needs improvement in X, Y. Let's discuss solutions"

### 5. Be Specific, Not Vague
**Avoid:** "You never do anything right"
**Use:** "The last three reports had formatting issues"

---

## Transformation Templates

### Insults → Constructive Feedback
| Toxic | Professional |
|-------|--------------|
| "You're an idiot" | "I think there's been a misunderstanding" |
| "This is garbage" | "This doesn't meet our quality standards" |
| "What's wrong with you?" | "Can we discuss what happened?" |

### Threats → Clear Consequences
| Toxic | Professional |
|-------|--------------|
| "You'll regret this" | "This decision may have consequences we should discuss" |
| "I'll destroy you" | "I will need to escalate this formally" |
| "Watch your back" | "I have concerns about how this is being handled" |

### Dismissiveness → Respectful Disagreement
| Toxic | Professional |
|-------|--------------|
| "Nobody cares" | "Let's refocus on priorities" |
| "That's stupid" | "I see some challenges with that approach" |
| "Whatever" | "I appreciate your input, though I'm considering alternatives" |

## Context-Specific Guidelines

### Workplace Feedback
- **Start with positives** when possible
- **Be specific** about issues
- **Offer support** for improvement
- **Set clear expectations**

**Example:**
- ❌ "You're terrible at presentations"
- ✅ "Your presentations would benefit from clearer structure and more visual aids. I can share resources to help."

### Disagreements
- **Acknowledge** the other perspective
- **State your view** without attacking
- **Provide reasoning**
- **Invite dialogue**

**Example:**
- ❌ "That's the dumbest idea ever"
- ✅ "I have concerns about that approach. Here's my reasoning, but I'd like to hear your thoughts."

### Urgent Requests
- **State urgency clearly**
- **Acknowledge constraints**
- **Offer support**
- **Be realistic**

**Example:**
- ❌ "Drop everything NOW"
- ✅ "This is urgent and needs immediate attention. Do you need help reprioritizing?"

### Performance Issues
- **Document specific instances**
- **Focus on impact**
- **Create improvement plan**
- **Set timeline**

**Example:**
- ❌ "You're useless at your job"
- ✅ "I've noticed recurring issues with X. Let's create a 30-day improvement plan with clear milestones."

## Red Flags to Avoid

### Never Professional:
- ❌ All-caps yelling
- ❌ Personal insults about appearance, identity, background
- ❌ Profanity
- ❌ Threats of violence or career destruction
- ❌ Discriminatory language
- ❌ Gaslighting ("that never happened")
- ❌ Public humiliation
- ❌ Absolute language ("you always", "you never")

### Sometimes Necessary But Use Carefully:
- ⚠️ Ultimatums (use only for serious, documented issues)
- ⚠️ Strong disagreement (be respectful, provide reasoning)
- ⚠️ Escalation (follow proper channels, be factual)

## Tone Calibration Scale

### Level 1: Neutral Observation
"I noticed X"

### Level 2: Gentle Concern
"I'm concerned about X"

### Level 3: Clear Feedback
"This doesn't meet standards"

### Level 4: Serious Issue
"This is a significant problem we must address"

### Level 5: Formal Consequence
"Continued issues will result in [specific consequence]"

**Match level to situation severity**

## Email-Specific Guidelines

### Opening
- ❌ "Per my last email which you ignored"
- ✅ "Following up on my previous message"

### Body
- ❌ "As I've told you multiple times"
- ✅ "To reiterate for clarity"

### Closing
- ❌ "Maybe you'll understand this time"
- ✅ "Please let me know if you need clarification"

---

## Meeting Language

### Interruptions
- ❌ "Stop talking"
- ✅ "Can I add a point here?"

### Disagreement
- ❌ "That's wrong"
- ✅ "I have a different perspective"

### Redirecting
- ❌ "This is off-topic, let's move on"
- ✅ "This is important but outside our agenda. Can we schedule separate time?"

---

## Cultural Considerations

### Global Teams
- Avoid idioms that don't translate
- Be explicit rather than indirect
- Acknowledge different communication norms
- Verify understanding

### Generational Differences
- Respect varied communication preferences
- Be flexible in medium (email, chat, call)
- Avoid stereotyping by age

### Neurodiversity
- Be clear and direct
- Provide written follow-up
- Allow processing time
- Respect different working styles

## Recovery from Toxic Communication

### If You Sent Toxic Message:
1. **Acknowledge quickly**: "I apologize for my tone in that email"
2. **Take responsibility**: Don't blame stress/circumstances
3. **Restate professionally**: "What I meant to say was..."
4. **Commit to improvement**: "I'll be more mindful going forward"

### If You Received Toxic Message:
1. **Don't mirror the toxicity**
2. **Address behavior, not person**: "I found that message disrespectful"
3. **Request professional communication**: "Can we restart this conversation with mutual respect?"
4. **Escalate if needed**: Document and involve appropriate parties

## Final Checklist Before Sending

✅ Does this preserve the core message?
✅ Would I say this face-to-face in an office?
✅ Is this specific and actionable?
✅ Does this focus on behavior, not person?
✅ Have I offered support or next steps?
✅ Is my tone proportional to the issue?
✅ Would HR approve this message?

If any answer is "no" - revise before sending.
